To export your Trading 212 data, open the **Menu** in your Invest or Stocks ISA account, go to **History** and tap the **export icon** in the top-right corner. Choose a date range of up to 12 months, tick the data you want (orders, dividends, transactions, interest), confirm, and download the CSV when it is ready. For a longer history, repeat the export for each 12-month period.

---

## Where is the export button in Trading 212?

The export lives on the History screen: **Menu → History → export icon** (top right). This is the path in [Trading 212's own help centre article on exporting data](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016898917-Can-I-export-the-trading-data-from-my-account), and it is the same in the mobile app and on the web platform.

It is not under Settings. If you go looking in the account settings or profile screens you will not find it. Start from History.

> **Quick check:** if you hold both an Invest account and a Stocks ISA, make sure the account switcher shows the right one before you open History. Each account exports separately.

## How do I export from the Trading 212 mobile app?

Open History from the Menu, tap the export icon and request the file. Step by step:

1. Open the Trading 212 app and switch to the account you want (Invest or Stocks ISA).
2. Tap **Menu**, then **History**.
3. Tap the **export icon** in the top-right corner of the History screen.
4. Set the **start and end date**. The range can be at most 12 months.
5. Tick the data types you want included, such as orders, dividends, transactions and interest.
6. Confirm the export. Trading 212 prepares the file and lets you know when it is ready to download.
7. Download the `.csv` and save it somewhere you can find it.

The file opens in Google Sheets, Excel or Numbers, or you can [upload your export to Vizvest](/dashboard) to see it as a dashboard.

## How do I export from the Trading 212 web platform?

The web platform uses the same path: Menu, History, then the export icon. Step by step:

1. Log in at [trading212.com](https://www.trading212.com) and select the account you want.
2. Open the **Menu** and choose **History**.
3. Click the **export icon** at the top right of the History view.
4. Choose a date range of up to 12 months and the data types to include.
5. Confirm, then download the CSV once it has been generated.

Mobile and web produce the same CSV format, so use whichever is more convenient.

> **Note:** these steps cover Invest and Stocks ISA accounts. CFD accounts have their own export, described in a [separate help centre article](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/36243765206301-How-to-export-the-trading-data-from-my-CFD-account). Vizvest is built for Invest and ISA exports.

## What data can you export from Trading 212?

You can export your orders, dividends, account transactions and interest. You choose which of these go into the file before you confirm.

| Data type | What it includes |
|---|---|
| **Orders** | Every buy and sell, with shares, price, exchange rate, result and any fees or stamp duty |
| **Dividends** | Each dividend payment, with the withholding tax taken at source |
| **Transactions** | Deposits, withdrawals and other cash movements |
| **Interest** | Interest paid on uninvested cash |

If you only want dividend data, untick everything else. For a portfolio tracker, include everything: missing deposits or orders make your totals look wrong.

## How do I export more than 12 months of Trading 212 history?

You can't do it in one file. Each export is capped at 12 months, so you run one export per 12-month period and combine the files afterwards. Trading 212's help centre describes the same approach for CFD accounts: [up to 12 months per file, and consecutive 12-month periods for anything longer](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/36243765206301-How-to-export-the-trading-data-from-my-CFD-account). Invest and ISA exports work the same way.

**Worked example.** Say you opened your account in March 2022 and today is 17 November 2026. Five exports cover everything:

| Export | Start date | End date |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 Mar 2022 | 28 Feb 2023 |
| 2 | 1 Mar 2023 | 29 Feb 2024 |
| 3 | 1 Mar 2024 | 28 Feb 2025 |
| 4 | 1 Mar 2025 | 28 Feb 2026 |
| 5 | 1 Mar 2026 | 17 Nov 2026 |

That is five files, each no longer than 12 months, with no gaps between them. Calendar years (1 January to 31 December) work just as well and are easier to remember. What matters is that the ranges touch end to end.

You can then upload all the files to Vizvest together. It merges them into one history and removes any rows that appear in two files, so a small overlap between ranges does no harm. A gap is the real problem.

> **Why full history matters:** if your oldest buys are missing, any profit and loss figures built from the file will be off. Vizvest warns you when it sees sells with no matching buys, which usually means the export window was too short.

## What does each column in the Trading 212 CSV mean?

Each row is one event (a trade, dividend, deposit and so on), and each column describes one part of it. The core columns are below. Vizvest reads every one of these.

| Column | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Action** | Type of event, for example `Market buy`, `Limit sell`, `Deposit`, `Withdrawal`, `Dividend (Dividend)` or `Interest on cash` |
| **Time** | Date and time of the event |
| **ISIN** | International Securities Identification Number of the share or ETF |
| **Ticker** | Ticker symbol, for example AAPL or VUSA |
| **Name** | Full name of the security |
| **Notes** | Extra context Trading 212 adds to some rows |
| **ID** | Trading 212's reference for the transaction |
| **No. of shares** | Quantity bought, sold or held for a dividend, including fractions |
| **Price / share** | Price per share, in the share's own currency |
| **Currency (Price / share)** | Currency of that price (USD, GBP, GBX for pence, EUR and so on) |
| **Exchange rate** | Rate used to convert from the share's currency to your account currency |
| **Result** | Realised profit or loss on a sell, worked out by Trading 212 |
| **Currency (Result)** | Currency of the Result figure |
| **Total** | Total cash that moved for the row, including fees |
| **Currency (Total)** | Currency of the Total, normally your account currency |
| **Withholding tax** | Tax deducted at source on a dividend |
| **Currency (Withholding tax)** | Currency of the withholding tax, usually the share's own currency |
| **Currency conversion fee** | Foreign exchange fee charged on the row, if any |
| **Currency (Currency conversion fee)** | Currency of that fee |

### Which other cost columns can also appear?

Your file can also contain extra cost columns, filled in only on rows where that cost applied. Each has a matching `Currency (...)` column:

| Column | When you see it |
|---|---|
| **Stamp duty reserve tax** | Buying UK shares (UK stamp duty on share purchases) |
| **French transaction tax** | Buying certain large French shares |
| **Charge amount** | Other charges on a transaction, in some exports |
| **Deposit fee** | A fee on a deposit, in some exports |

Trading 212 has changed the column set and order over time, so two files from different years may not line up exactly. These cost columns are already included in **Total**, so don't add them on top when you sum the cash you spent. For what each charge is and when it applies, see our guide to [Trading 212 fees](/articles/trading-212-fees-explained).

The columns that matter most for analysis are **Action**, **Time**, **Ticker**, **No. of shares**, **Price / share**, **Exchange rate**, **Result**, **Total** and **Withholding tax**.

## Why does my Trading 212 export look wrong?

Most problems come from a missing data type, a short date range or how the file was opened. Here are the common ones.

### Why are my dividends missing?

The Dividends option was probably not ticked when you exported. Run the export again with it selected.

### Why won't the file open properly in Excel?

Double-clicking can garble characters such as £, € or accented company names. In Excel, use **Data → From Text/CSV** and choose UTF-8 so the file is read correctly.

### Why is my profit and loss wrong after uploading?

You almost certainly have a partial history. If you bought a share before the start of your export, the buy is not in the file. Export every 12-month period back to your first deposit and upload all the files together.

### Why can't I find the export button?

You are probably looking in Settings. The export icon is on the **History** screen (Menu → History, top right). If it still isn't there, update the app or use the web platform.

### Why is the exchange rate column blank or 1?

The trade was already in your account currency, so no conversion was needed.

## What can I do with my exported Trading 212 data?

The CSV is raw data. To get totals, charts and per-stock breakdowns you either build them yourself in a spreadsheet or use a tool that reads the file.

- **A spreadsheet**: flexible and free, but you build every formula and pivot table yourself.
- **Vizvest**: [upload your Trading 212 CSV](/dashboard) to see realised profit per position, dividends by month and quarter net of withholding tax, your currency conversion fees and withholding tax totals, allocation and your trading activity. It is free, needs no account, and the file is processed in your browser and never uploaded.
- **A paid tracker**: tools such as Sharesight import Trading 212 data and add features like live prices. We compare the options in our [Trading 212 portfolio tracker guide](/articles/trading-212-portfolio-tracker).

If you mainly care about income, our [Trading 212 dividend tracker guide](/articles/trading-212-dividend-tracker) shows how to turn the dividend rows into a yearly income figure.

## Summary

- The export is at **Menu → History → export icon**, not in Settings, on both mobile and web.
- Each export covers at most 12 months. Run one per period, with no gaps, for your full history.
- Export Invest and Stocks ISA accounts separately.
- Besides the core columns, files can include stamp duty, French transaction tax, currency conversion fee and other charge columns. These are already inside **Total**.
- Upload several files to [Vizvest](/dashboard) at once and it merges them and removes duplicates.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the export button in Trading 212?

Open the Menu in your Invest or Stocks ISA account, choose History, then tap the export icon in the top-right corner of the History screen. Pick a date range and the data types you want, confirm, and download the CSV once Trading 212 has prepared it.

### Can I export more than 12 months of Trading 212 data at once?

No. Each export covers at most 12 months. For a longer history, run one export per 12-month period with no gaps between them, then combine the files. Vizvest accepts several CSVs at once, merges them and removes duplicate rows.

### What file format does Trading 212 export?

A CSV (comma-separated values) file. It opens in Excel, Google Sheets or Numbers, and it is the format most portfolio trackers import.

### Do I need to export my ISA and Invest accounts separately?

Yes. Each account has its own history, so switch to the account you want before you open History and export. Repeat the process for the other account.

### Does the Trading 212 export include fractional shares?

Yes. The No. of shares column records fractional quantities, for example 0.4127 shares, exactly as they were bought or sold.

### Why are my dividends missing from my Trading 212 export?

The Dividends option was probably not ticked when you requested the export. Run the export again for the same date range with Dividends selected. Each payment then appears as its own dividend row, with any withholding tax in its own column.
