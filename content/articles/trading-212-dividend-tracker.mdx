A Trading 212 dividend tracker takes the dividend rows from your Trading 212 CSV export and adds them up by month, quarter, year and stock, showing the tax withheld at source. Trading 212 lists each payment in your History but gives no full income breakdown. Export your history with dividends included, then upload it to a tracker or a spreadsheet.

## How do dividends work on Trading 212?

When a company or fund you hold pays a dividend, Trading 212 credits it to your account, net of any tax withheld at source. This works the same in the **Invest** account and the **Stocks ISA**.

Each payment appears in your History and in the CSV export as its own row. In the export, a dividend row carries:

- the **Action**, such as `Dividend (Dividend)`
- the **No. of shares** you held and the dividend **Price / share**
- the **Withholding tax** deducted at source, and its currency
- the **Exchange rate** used to convert into your account currency
- the **Total** credited to your account, after withholding tax

Trading 212 is a big platform, so a lot of people need this. In May 2025 it said it had passed [£25 billion in client assets and around 4.5 million clients](https://www.trading212.com/legal-documentation/Trading-212-Press-release-21.05.2025_EN.pdf).

### Are Trading 212 dividends reinvested automatically?

Only if you have switched reinvestment on. Trading 212 explains the options in its help centre articles on [how you receive dividends](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/360007327218-How-can-I-receive-dividends-Invest-Stocks-ISA) and [Pie dividend reinvesting](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/30673839245981-Pie-Dividend-reinvesting-DRIP).

- **Reinvestment on:** the dividend is used to buy more shares, including fractions.
- **Reinvestment off:** the dividend arrives as cash and waits in your account until you invest it.
- **Holdings in a Pie:** reinvested dividends go towards the Pie's target allocation, not necessarily back into the stock that paid them.

The app's behaviour has changed over time, so check the setting in your own account rather than assuming. Either way, the dividend still shows up as a dividend row in your export, so your income total is not affected.

## What dividend data does Trading 212 show you, and what is missing?

Trading 212 shows each payment as it arrives, but not the totals and trends a dividend investor needs.

| Question | In the Trading 212 app? |
|---|---|
| Which stock paid me, and how much? | Yes, one payment at a time in History |
| How much did I get this month, quarter or year? | Not as a total |
| Which holdings pay me the most over time? | No |
| Is my income growing year on year? | No |
| How much withholding tax was taken in total? | No |

Every one of those answers is in your CSV export. The data is there; it just needs adding up.

## How do I track my Trading 212 dividends?

Export your history with dividends included, then total it in a spreadsheet or a dividend tracker. Here is the quickest route.

1. **Export your history.** In Trading 212, go to **Menu → History** and tap the export icon. Choose a range of up to 12 months and make sure **Dividends** is ticked. Repeat for each 12-month period you want. The full walkthrough is in [how to export your Trading 212 data](/articles/how-to-export-trading-212-data).
2. **Upload the files.** [Upload your Trading 212 CSV to Vizvest](/dashboard). You can drop in several files at once; Vizvest merges them and removes duplicate rows. Nothing is uploaded to a server.
3. **Open the Dividends view.** It shows your dividend income laid out by period and by stock.

### What does the Vizvest dividend view show?

The Dividends view in Vizvest shows:

- **Gross dividends**, **withholding tax** and **net dividends** (what actually reached your account)
- the number of **payments** and the **average per payment**
- **income over time** as a running total
- **dividends by month or by quarter**
- a **year-over-year** comparison
- **income by stock**, so you can see which holdings pay you most

All amounts are converted to your account's base currency at the exchange rate recorded in your CSV.

## What does a year of dividend tracking look like?

Here is an example. Say your 2025 export contains these dividend rows (figures are illustrative, already converted to pounds):

| Holding | Payments in year | Gross | Withholding tax | Net received |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US stock A | 4 | £120.00 | £18.00 | £102.00 |
| US stock B | 4 | £80.00 | £12.00 | £68.00 |
| UK stock C | 2 | £150.00 | £0.00 | £150.00 |
| Ireland-domiciled ETF D | 4 | £90.00 | £0.00 | £90.00 |
| **Total** | **14** | **£440.00** | **£30.00** | **£410.00** |

From this you can read off that:

- net dividend income for the year was **£410**, or about **£34 a month**
- withholding tax took **£30**, all of it from the two US stocks
- the UK stock paid twice a year (interim and final), while the US stocks and ETF paid quarterly

In this example the UK stock and the ETF show nothing in the **Withholding tax** column, so their gross and net figures are the same. Your own export shows the exact amount deducted on every payment, and Vizvest totals it next to your gross and net income.

> Vizvest doesn't cover tax. For anything tax-related, speak to a qualified tax adviser.

## What is the difference between dividend yield and yield on cost?

Dividend yield compares a year's dividends with today's share price. Yield on cost compares them with the price you paid. Both are easy to work out yourself.

- **Dividend yield** = annual dividend per share ÷ current share price. A share at £20 paying £1 a year yields 5%. If the price falls and the dividend holds, the yield rises, so a high yield is not automatically good news.
- **Yield on cost** = annual dividend per share ÷ your average purchase price. If you bought at £10 and it now pays £1 a year, your yield on cost is 10%, whatever the price does today.

Your CSV gives you the dividends received and your buy prices, so you can calculate yield on cost in a spreadsheet. Vizvest does not calculate yield on cost or dividend yield, because it has no live price feed; it focuses on the income you actually received.

## How do I project my future dividend income?

Start with last year's net income and compound it at a growth rate you think is realistic. This is a simple calculation you can do in a spreadsheet.

1. Take your total net dividends for the last full year (Vizvest shows this in the year-over-year view).
2. Pick a growth rate for your holdings' dividends. Be conservative.
3. Compound forward: **income × (1 + growth rate)^years**.

**Example:** £2,400 a year in dividends growing at 5% a year becomes about £3,909 a year after 10 years (£2,400 × 1.05¹⁰), before any new money you add. Dividends can be cut as well as raised, so treat any projection as a rough guide.

## When do Trading 212 dividends arrive during the year?

It depends on what you hold. Many US companies pay quarterly, many UK companies pay twice a year (an interim and a final dividend), and ETFs vary from monthly to annually.

To see your own pattern, look at the **Time** column on your dividend rows, or switch the Vizvest dividend chart between months and quarters. If most of your income lands in a few months, you can see that at a glance.

For the cost side, including the currency conversion fee on US shares, see [Trading 212 fees explained](/articles/trading-212-fees-explained). And if you want more than dividends, our [Trading 212 portfolio tracker comparison](/articles/trading-212-portfolio-tracker) looks at the wider options.
