Trading 212 currency affects your returns in two ways. First, a 0.15% FX fee applies when you trade an instrument in a different currency from the one you pay with. Second, the exchange rate moves while you hold, so a US stock can rise in dollars and still lose money in pounds. Your account currency, the instrument's currency and the rate between them all shape the number you see.

---

## What is the difference between account currency and instrument currency?

Your account currency (Trading 212 calls it the primary currency) is the one you chose when you opened the account. The instrument currency is the one a share or ETF is priced in on its exchange. When the two differ, every trade involves a currency conversion.

Trading 212's help centre says the [primary currency can't be changed](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/360007308078-Can-I-change-my-account-s-primary-currency) once the account is created, and your portfolio, orders, results and transactions are all displayed in it.

| You hold | Account currency | Instrument currency | Conversion needed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple (Nasdaq) | GBP | USD | Yes |
| A UK share on the London Stock Exchange | GBP | GBX (pence) | No, pence and pounds are the same currency |
| A London-listed ETF priced in pounds | GBP | GBP | No |
| A London-listed ETF priced in dollars | GBP | USD | Yes |
| A German share on Xetra | GBP | EUR | Yes |

The listing matters, not the company. Two ETFs tracking the same index can be priced in different currencies on the same exchange, and only one of them needs a conversion from a pound account.

## How does the Trading 212 FX fee work?

Trading 212 charges **0.15%** whenever it converts currency for you. On a trade, that means buying or selling an instrument priced in a different currency from the one you pay with. The [fees page for Invest, ISA and SIPP accounts](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/11471996799517-What-are-the-fees-in-the-Invest-ISAs-and-SIPP) lists it as the only fee Trading 212 itself charges on those accounts.

According to Trading 212's [FX fee article](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/360018909758-What-is-the-FX-fee-Invest-Stocks-ISA), the trade value is converted at the spot exchange rate adjusted by 0.15%. Its example uses a GBP/USD spot rate of 1.4000/1.4100: on a buy, your pounds convert at 1.3979 dollars instead of 1.40. The key points:

- **It applies both ways.** You pay it on the buy and again on the sell, so a round trip costs about 0.3%.
- **Pies and AutoInvest pay it too.** Trading 212 confirms the fee applies to [orders placed within pies](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/360018769897-Is-there-an-FX-Conversion-Fee-for-AutoInvest) that hold instruments in other currencies.
- **Dividends are exempt.** Payments from corporate events, such as dividends, don't carry the fee.
- **You see it first.** The FX fee is shown on the Review Order screen before you confirm.

On a £2,400 purchase of a US stock, 0.15% is about £3.60 on the way in, and a similar amount on the way out. Our guide to [Trading 212 fees](/articles/trading-212-fees-explained) covers the other costs that can apply to a trade.

## How do exchange rates affect your Trading 212 returns?

On a foreign share, your return in pounds is the share's return in its own currency plus or minus the change in the exchange rate. If the pound strengthens while you hold a US stock, your dollars buy fewer pounds when you sell. If the pound weakens, the currency adds to your gain.

### Worked example: up 8% in dollars, down in pounds

This is an illustration with made-up round numbers. You have a GBP account and buy 20 shares of a US company at $150.

1. **Buy.** 20 × $150 = $3,000. At a GBP/USD rate of 1.25, that costs £2,400, plus an FX fee of about £3.60.
2. **Hold.** The share price rises to $162. In dollars, your shares are worth $3,240, up 8%.
3. **The pound strengthens.** By the time you sell, GBP/USD is 1.36.
4. **Sell.** $3,240 ÷ 1.36 = £2,382.35, less an FX fee of about £3.57.

Before fees, you're down **£17.65**. After both FX fees, the loss is about **£24.82**, on a share that went up 8%.

Trading 212's [return calculation article](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/11703149198237-How-are-my-return-and-gain-loss-calculated-Invest-Stocks-ISA) splits the Return on a position into two parts: Return = Gain/Loss + FX impact. Applied to this example (before fees):

- **Gain/Loss** (the price move at today's rate): ($162 − $150) × 20 ÷ 1.36 = **+£176.47**
- **FX impact** (the rate move on your original $3,000): $3,000 ÷ 1.36 − £2,400 = **−£194.12**
- **Return:** **−£17.65**

The same trade gives a very different result in pounds depending on where the exchange rate ends up:

| GBP/USD when you sell | Sale value in £ | Return in £ (before fees) | Return in % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.15 (pound weaker) | £2,817.39 | +£417.39 | +17.4% |
| 1.25 (unchanged) | £2,592.00 | +£192.00 | +8.0% |
| 1.36 (pound stronger) | £2,382.35 | −£17.65 | −0.7% |

In every row the share did exactly the same thing. Only the currency changed.

## Why is my Trading 212 return different in GBP?

Because your account reports everything in your primary currency, while the share moves in its own. The Return you see on a US holding includes the effect of GBP/USD since you bought, so it won't match the share's percentage move in dollars.

Trading 212's article [Why is the price higher, but my return's negative?](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/360008141258-Why-is-the-price-higher-but-my-return-s-negative-Currency-Depreciation) explains how to see the split for an open position:

1. Open the position.
2. Scroll down to the **Your investment** section.
3. Tap **Return** to see the breakdown, including the FX part.

The same article points out that currency markets and stock markets keep different hours. Your Return on a US share can move overnight or at the weekend even though the share price hasn't, because GBP/USD is still trading.

Other common reasons your figure differs from a dollar chart:

- **FX fees.** About 0.15% each way on foreign trades.
- **Different reference points.** A share chart shows the price move. Your Return is measured against your average price and the exchange rates at the times you bought.
- **Several buys at different rates.** If you bought in stages, each buy had its own exchange rate, so your FX starting point is a blend of them.

For how realised and unrealised results fit together, see [Trading 212 profit and loss explained](/articles/trading-212-profit-and-loss-explained).

## Are UK shares on Trading 212 priced in pence or pounds?

Most London-listed shares are priced in pence, shown as **GBX** (sometimes GBp), not pounds (GBP). A price of 412.50 GBX means £4.125 per share. Divide by 100 to get pounds.

This catches people out in two places:

- **In the app,** a UK share priced at 412.50 looks like it costs hundreds of pounds. It doesn't.
- **In your CSV export,** `Price / share` is in the instrument's currency, so UK share rows show pence with `GBX` in the `Currency (Price / share)` column. `Total` is in your account currency.

Example: you buy 50 shares at 412.50 GBX. That's 50 × 412.50 = 20,625 pence, or **£206.25**. The `Total` on that row will be around £206.25, plus any charges on the trade.

Pence and pounds are the same currency, so a pence-priced UK share bought from a GBP account involves no currency conversion and no FX fee. Some London-listed ETFs are priced in GBP or USD instead of pence, so check the currency on each instrument rather than assuming.

## What is a Trading 212 multi-currency account?

It's a feature that lets you hold cash in more than one currency and trade in it directly. Trading 212's [multi-currency article](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/11669719976093-What-is-a-multi-currency-account) says it's available on **Invest** and **SIPP** accounts only. Stocks ISA and CFD accounts use the primary currency for all transactions.

How it works, according to Trading 212's help centre:

- **Supported currencies** are GBP, USD, EUR, CHF, DKK, NOK, PLN, SEK, CZK, RON, HUF, CAD and AUD. What's available can depend on your country of residence.
- **Converting cash costs 0.15%.** You can convert between balances instantly, including at weekends, with a limit of 100 conversion requests per 24 hours.
- **Trading in the asset's currency has no FX fee.** When you place an order you can [choose which currency to pay with](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/34159237080861-Can-I-choose-the-currency-in-which-to-buy-sell-assets-for-Invest-accounts). Pick USD for a US stock and there's no conversion on that trade. You can set a default under More → Settings → Trading preferences → Currency options.
- **Pies still use your primary currency.** Money going into or out of a pie comes from your primary balance, so foreign instruments in a pie are converted at the 0.15% fee, including dividend reinvestments.
- **Dividends arrive in your primary currency**, with no FX fee.
- **Your account value and statements** are still shown in your primary currency.
- **If a balance is short,** Trading 212 checks the trade's currency first, then your primary currency, then [the currency with the most free funds](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/17861030897821-What-happens-if-there-aren-t-enough-funds-in-the-main-currency). One order always uses one currency.

### Does a multi-currency account remove currency risk?

No. It removes the **fee** on each trade, not the **exchange rate risk**. If you convert £2,400 into dollars and buy a US stock, you're exposed to GBP/USD from the moment you convert, not from the moment you buy. Your account value is still reported in pounds, so a stronger pound still shrinks it.

Where it helps is frequent trading. Say you make ten round trips of £2,400 in US shares in a year:

| | Paying from GBP each time | Converting once to USD |
|---|---|---|
| FX fees on trades | 10 × about £7.20 = about £72 | £0 |
| FX fee on conversion | £0 | about £3.60 (plus about £3.60 if you convert back) |
| Exchange rate exposure | Yes | Yes |

If you buy a US stock and hold it for years, the difference is small. If you trade the same currency often, holding that currency saves most of the fees. If you sell in the instrument's currency, Trading 212 says you realise the market part of the result without FX impact or the FX fee at that point, but the dollars you hold still move against the pound.

## How does the Trading 212 CSV record currency?

Each row in your export carries its own currency information: the price in the instrument's currency, the exchange rate used, and the total in your account currency. Column names have changed between export versions, but these are the ones that matter:

| Column | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `Price / share` | Price per share in the instrument's own currency |
| `Currency (Price / share)` | That currency: USD, EUR, GBX for pence and so on |
| `Exchange rate` | The rate between the instrument's currency and your account currency for that row |
| `Total` | Cash that moved, in your account currency, including fees on the row |
| `Currency (Total)` | Normally your account currency |
| `Result` | Trading 212's profit or loss on a sell |
| `Currency (Result)` | The currency of the Result |
| `Currency conversion fee` | The FX fee charged on that row, if any |
| `Currency (Currency conversion fee)` | The fee's currency |

In practice the exchange rate is expressed as units of the instrument's currency per one unit of your account currency, so you can check a row like this:

`No. of shares` × `Price / share` ÷ `Exchange rate` ≈ `Total` − fees

For the worked example above, an illustrative buy row would read: 20 shares, `Price / share` 150.00 USD, `Exchange rate` 1.25, `Currency conversion fee` 3.60 GBP and `Total` 2,403.60 GBP. That's $3,000 ÷ 1.25 = £2,400, plus the £3.60 fee. Every buy and sell has its own rate, which is why your FX starting point on a position you built over time is a blend.

## How does Vizvest put every currency into one total?

Vizvest picks one base currency and converts each row into it using the exchange rate already in your CSV. It doesn't fetch outside rates, so your totals match the rates Trading 212 actually used.

When you [upload your Trading 212 export to Vizvest](/dashboard), it:

1. **Detects your base currency** as the most common value in the `Currency (Total)` column. For most people that's simply their account currency.
2. **Keeps rows already in that currency as they are.** Because `Total` is in your account currency, pence-priced UK shares and dollar-priced US shares are added up together in pounds.
3. **Converts any row whose total is in another currency** by dividing it by that row's `Exchange rate`.
4. **Asks you to choose** if you upload several files that use different base currencies, for example exports from two accounts.

From there, buy and sell volume, deposits, dividends and realised profit (from the `Result` column) all appear in one currency. The overview adds up the `Currency conversion fee` column into a single currency fees figure, so you can see what conversions have cost you over your whole history. Transaction tables still show each share's price in its own currency, with pence marked as p.

Vizvest runs entirely in your browser. Your file is never uploaded or stored, and refreshing the page clears it. It has no live prices, so it can't show the current value or unrealised FX impact of positions you still hold. Use the Return breakdown in the Trading 212 app for those. If you hold pies with foreign slices, our [Trading 212 pie performance guide](/articles/trading-212-pie-performance) shows how to review them.

## Summary

- Your **account currency** is fixed. When a share is priced in a different **instrument currency**, every trade involves a conversion.
- The Trading 212 **FX fee is 0.15%** on each conversion, on buys and sells, including pies. Dividends are exempt.
- On foreign shares, **Return = Gain/Loss + FX impact**. A US stock can be up in dollars and down in pounds.
- UK shares are usually priced in **pence (GBX)**. Divide by 100 for pounds. Your CSV's `Total` is already in your account currency.
- A **multi-currency** Invest or SIPP account cuts FX fees for frequent traders but doesn't remove exchange rate risk. The Stocks ISA uses the primary currency only.
- [Vizvest](/dashboard) converts every row of your export to one base currency at the rate in the file, and totals your currency conversion fees.
